## From Pixel Cells to a 3D Prison Sandbox

The headline change is perspective. Earlier games in the franchise were built around 2D pixel art, where a tunnel was a line on a map and a guard was a sprite with a patrol pattern. The Escapists 3 moves the whole concept into a 3D sandbox, which changes how you read a prison.

| Element | Earlier Escapists games | The Escapists 3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visual style | 2D pixel art | 3D sandbox |
| Escape routes | Dig, craft, disguise | Dig, sneak, climb, fight, disguise, improvise |
| Prison count | Multiple themed facilities | Five distinctive prisons |
| Multiplayer | Co-op escapes | 2 local split-screen, up to 4 online |
| Progression | Per-prison learning | XP, unlockable skills carried between prisons |

In practice, a 3D layout means verticality matters. Climbing routes, sight lines, and the physical space between your cell and the perimeter all become part of the puzzle rather than abstract map coordinates. Community reports from the reveal trailer suggest the shift is the biggest talking point among long-time players, and it is the main reason the Escapists 3 Steam listing has been drawing attention before any gameplay footage of a full prison has surfaced.

## Five Prisons and the World Beyond the Walls

Team17 is promising five distinctive prisons, each with its own routines, characters, and secrets. Because every facility runs on a schedule, the intended loop is observation first: learn when guards patrol, when roll call happens, and where the system is softest. The page frames each prison as "a system waiting to be understood," which is a polite way of saying it is waiting to be exploited.

| Escape approach | What it involves | Best suited to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dig beneath the walls | Tunneling out from your cell over multiple in-game days | Patient, low-risk planners |
| Climb your way out | Finding vertical routes past fences and walls | Players who like scouting |
| Disguise yourself | Blending in with staff or visitors | Stealth-focused inmates |
| Fight your way out | Direct confrontation with guards | Players who enjoy chaos |
| Improvise | Combining crafted junk into an unorthodox plan | Creative problem solvers |

Getting past the perimeter is only half the job. The page hints that each prison opens onto a different exterior — dense forests, gusty canyons, ancient catacombs, and dangerous waters all appear in the description. That suggests escape is now a two-stage problem: break out, then survive the terrain on the other side.

## Crafting: 120+ Items, Weapons, and Bad Ideas

Crafting remains the engine of the whole experience. The listing advertises more than 120 items and weapons to loot and craft, and almost anything can theoretically become part of an escape plan — whether it should is a different question entirely.

- **Search cells** for raw materials left lying around
- **Trade with inmates** to fill gaps in your inventory
- **Complete prison jobs** to earn cash and access
- **Combine junk** into tools, weapons, and disguises
- **Upgrade your inmate** with XP earned along the way

The progression system is worth noting. You earn XP as you play, unlock new skills, and tailor your character toward the escape style you prefer. Crucially, what you learn in one prison carries into the next, so a failed tunnel attempt is not wasted time — it is research.
