## The Escapists Series Timeline: Why the Gap Matters

## Where The Escapists 3 Release Date Rumors Come From

Most fake dates follow a handful of recognizable patterns. Knowing them saves you from getting excited about nothing.

**Table 3: Common Sources of False Release Dates**

| Source Type | Typical Claim | Reliability |
|---|---|---|
| Community wiki pages | "Cancelled" or "TBA" entries | Very low — fan-edited |
| YouTube thumbnails | "RELEASE DATE CONFIRMED!" | Very low — clickbait |
| AI-written blog spam | Invented dates and prices | Very low — no sourcing |
| Fake countdown sites | A precise date and timer | Very low — engagement bait |
| Retail placeholder listings | "December 31" style dates | Low — placeholders, not real |
| Publisher financial reports | Revenue projections only | Medium — indirect hints |
| Official Team17 channels | Direct confirmation | High — authoritative |

One important tell: if a date is a round number like December 31 or a first-of-the-month, it's almost certainly a placeholder that retailers use when they don't know the real date.

Another tell is specificity without a source. A legitimate announcement includes a trailer, a store page, and a press release — all at once. A rumor includes a date and nothing else.

### The "Cancelled" Label Trap

Some fan wikis categorize unannounced sequels as cancelled simply because no news exists. That's an assumption, not a fact. A game with no public announcement isn't cancelled — it's unannounced, which is a completely different thing. Treating the two as the same is how misinformation spreads.
