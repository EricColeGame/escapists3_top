## How Crafting Works in The Escapists 3

Prison walls don't fall to brute force in this series. They fall to a plastic spoon, a roll of duct tape, and a plan that started three in-game days earlier. That is exactly why a reliable The Escapists 3 crafting guide matters more than reflexes: crafting is the engine that turns scavenged junk into an actual escape route, and every other system in the game either feeds it or threatens it.

The Escapists 3 was announced on October 8, 2026, and is expected to launch in 2027 on PC, PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and Nintendo Switch 2. It moves the franchise into a 3D prison sandbox, but Team17 has not yet published an official item or recipe list — so this The Escapists 3 crafting guide focuses on the crafting loop itself, which is the part you can learn right now.

That loop has four stages, and each one carries its own failure mode.

| Stage | What You Do | Main Risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scavenge | Search desks, trade with inmates, collect during job shifts | Being spotted in restricted rooms |
| Store | Hide materials in your cell or an off-cell stash | Cell searches and shake-downs |
| Craft | Combine materials during private, unobserved moments | Carrying contraband through scan points |
| Deploy | Use the finished tool on your chosen route | Tool durability and guard patrols |

Most new players lose runs at stage three, not stage four. A crafted tool becomes contraband the instant it exists, so the moment of creation is also the moment of maximum exposure.

## The Five Item Categories and What They Unlock

Every craftable item in the series maps to one of five practical roles. Learning the categories is more useful than memorizing individual recipes, because categories survive roster changes and balance tweaks — and they are what you should actually plan around.

| Category | Typical Examples | Primary Use | Risk Level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Digging tools | Plastic Spoon, Fork, Shovel, Sturdy Shovel | Cut and extend tunnels | High |
| Wall-breaking tools | Crowbar, Pickaxe, Plastic Knife | Damage walls, grates, and vents | High |
| Repair and concealment | Duct Tape, Timber, Sheet of Metal, Paper Mache | Patch damage, reinforce routes, hide evidence | Medium |
| Disguises and access | Guard Outfit, Civilian Outfit, Key | Cross restricted areas and checkpoints | High |
| Distraction and cover | Bed Dummy, Dummy, Molten Chocolate | Cover an absence, pull guards off a route | Medium |

A few patterns are worth internalizing before you start hoarding materials:

- **Digging tools are slow to build and easy to detect.** Debris is evidence, and evidence gets inspections scheduled.
- **Repair and concealment items look passive but win runs.** They are the difference between a tunnel that survives a shakedown and one that gets patched over by guards.
- **Disguise items carry the highest instant risk.** Wearing the wrong outfit in the wrong zone is an immediate tell, no matter how good your timing is.
- **Distraction items buy time, not access.** They solve scheduling problems, not physical ones.

### Matching Categories to Escape Styles

- **Tunnel escapers:** digging tools first, concealment materials second, and debris hidden after every digging session.
- **Perimeter escapers:** cutting or climbing gear first, then fatigue and timing management built around patrol gaps.
- **Restricted-area escapers:** disguises and keys first, defensive gear second, and searches avoided at all costs.

**The route-first rule:** choose your escape route during early reconnaissance, then craft toward it. Tunnel builders almost never need a guard outfit, and disguise runners almost never need a pickaxe. Splitting your material budget across categories is the most common reason escape attempts quietly stall out.

### Why Tool Durability Decides Runs

Community reports from earlier entries point to the same recurring pattern: tools degrade as you use them, and a tool that snaps mid-tunnel leaves you with an open hole and no way to finish. Budget materials for a repair or a spare *before* you commit to the final run, not after.

### Intelligence and Recipe Unlocks

Player experience across The Escapists and The Escapists 2 suggests crafting quality and recipe availability are tied to your character's intelligence stat. Reading books and attending classes early means fewer wasted materials later, because you spend fewer attempts discovering which items combine with which. Treat intelligence training as a crafting investment, not a side activity.

## Step-by-Step Crafting Pipeline

Here is a preparation sequence that holds up under routine inspections. Follow it in order and you will rarely be caught holding something you should not have.

1. **Learn the schedule.** Attend roll call, meals, and work duties. Map the free-time windows when workshops and desks are searchable with minimal guard attention.
2. **Secure income and access.** Take a prison job and build inmate relationships. Cash and favors open supply channels that random desk searches can never match.
3. **Scavenge smart.** Search only during low-risk periods, take materials that fit your route, and leave junk behind. Every unnecessary item is contraband exposure.
4. **Craft in private.** Combine materials only where guards cannot observe you, then move the finished tool to a secure stash immediately.
5. **Prepare a backup.** Craft a spare or line up an alternate route before the final attempt.
6. **Run the pre-escape checklist.** Confirm timing, durability, stash security, and any disguise or distraction item your route requires.

| Phase | Focus | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–2 | Reconnaissance | Route chosen, schedule mapped |
| Days 3–4 | Income and access | Job secured, supply contacts established |
| Days 5–7 | Scavenging | Primary tool materials collected |
| Days 8–10 | Crafting | Primary tool built, stash secured off-cell |
| Final day | Backup and timing | Spare tool or alternate route ready |

## Contraband, Heat, and Search Survival

Crafting is only half the battle. Every tool you make is contraband, and the series punishes players who carry it carelessly. Heat is a resource you manage just like materials.

| Situation | Recommended Action | Consequence If Ignored |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine cell search expected | Move contraband to an off-cell stash | Items confiscated, heat rises |
| Passing through a scan point | Empty your pockets, use a hiding method | Immediate detection |
| Guard approaching while crafting | Cancel, stow materials, walk away calmly | Alert level increases |
| Tool durability running low | Repair or replace before the escape run | Tool failure mid-route |
| Unexplained wall damage discovered | Patch it with concealment materials | Prison placed on alert |

**Pre-escape crafting checklist:**

- Route reconnaissance finished and timing window identified
- Primary tool crafted and durability checked
- Backup tool or alternate route prepared
- Contraband stash secured away from your own cell
- Disguise or distraction item ready if the route calls for one

And the rule that saves the most runs: never craft early just for fun. An item you do not need adds risk without benefit, and every extra object in your inventory is a liability during a search.

## FAQ

**Q: Are the recipes in this The Escapists 3 crafting guide confirmed?**

No. The game is expected in 2027 and Team17 has not published an official recipe list. The categories and example items here reflect established systems from The Escapists and The Escapists 2, which the new game's crafting loop is built on. Confirmed recipes will be added once they are available.

**Q: What should I craft first?**

It depends entirely on your route. Tunnel escapes prioritize digging tools and concealment materials, perimeter escapes prioritize cutting or climbing gear, and restricted-area routes prioritize disguises and access items. Decide the route before you decide the recipe.

**Q: How do I keep crafted tools from being confiscated?**

Store contraband in spots guards rarely inspect, avoid carrying tools through scan points, and craft only during private moments in the daily schedule. Attending required activities keeps heat low, which reduces how often searches happen at all.

**Q: Does intelligence affect crafting?**

Community reports from earlier entries suggest it does, particularly around recipe discovery and tool effectiveness. Training intelligence early through reading and classes is a cheap way to avoid wasting scarce materials later.
