## The Daily Routine: How to Structure Your First Week

Every The Escapists 3 beginner guide worth reading starts with the same lesson: the schedule is not background flavor, it's the game. Roll call, meals, work, and lights out create windows of opportunity. Miss one and you spend the rest of the day dodging suspicion instead of making progress.

A typical prison day in the series looks roughly like this. Exact clock times vary by facility, so confirm them in-game on your first morning.

| Time Block | What Happens | Beginner Move |
|---|---|---|
| Morning roll call | Guards count every inmate | Stand on your cell marker, always |
| Breakfast | Food restores energy and stats | Eat every meal — it's free progress |
| Work period | Job shift for pay | Take a job early; income compounds |
| Free time | The only window for real progress | Dig, craft, trade, or exercise |
| Exercise period | Stat training | Use it for Strength or Speed |
| Evening roll call | Second headcount | Same discipline as the morning |
| Lights out | Guards thin out, lights dim | Craft and tunnel after dark |

The single biggest beginner mistake is treating free time as unlimited. It isn't. You get one meaningful block per day, and it has to cover digging, crafting, trading, and stat training. Pick one goal per day and finish it rather than starting four things you can't complete.

**Player experience** from earlier entries in the series suggests a simple priority order for week one: get a job, raise one stat to a useful threshold, then start collecting the materials for your chosen escape route. Inmates who rush straight to digging usually get caught during a random cell search before the tunnel is even halfway done.

## Stats, Skills, and Gear: What to Level First

This The Escapists 3 beginner guide recommends a simple rule: pick one stat and push it hard rather than spreading points evenly. Each stat unlocks a different category of escape, and a specialist finishes a route faster than a generalist.

| Stat | How to Raise It | What It Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| Strength | Push-ups, weights, physical jobs | Combat, heavy digging, moving obstacles |
| Speed | Running, treadmills, cardio | Outrunning guards, dodging, quick escapes |
| Intellect | Books, desk study, library time | Crafting recipes, cutting fences, electronics |

| Starter Item | Typical Use | How to Get It |
|---|---|---|
| Digging tool | Tunnel work under floors and walls | Crafted or traded from another inmate |
| Bed dummy | Covers your absence during roll call | Crafted from bedding and clothing |
| Guard outfit | Disguise for moving through restricted areas | Crafted or acquired through a favor |
| Cutters | Fences, bars, and chain-link | Crafted with Intellect |
| Stash bag | Hides contraband during searches | Crafted or bought with job income |

Intellect is usually the safest first investment because it opens up crafting, and crafting is what turns a random pile of junk into an escape kit. Strength is the better pick if you plan a brute-force route. Speed matters most if your plan involves being somewhere you shouldn't be and leaving quickly.

One more piece of gear advice: build your decoy before your tunnel, not after. A bed dummy takes materials and time, and players who leave it to the last minute almost always get caught at the first headcount after they disappear.

## Heat, Guards, and Staying Under the Radar

The Escapists 3 beginner guide advice that saves the most runs is simple: keep your heat low. Heat is the invisible meter tracking how much attention you've drawn — contraband found, fights started, areas trespassed. When it climbs, guards search more often, and random searches are what kill unfinished escapes.

| Heat Level | Likely Consequence | What to Do |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Guards ignore you | Keep working; stay boring |
| Medium | More frequent cell searches | Use your stash, carry nothing risky |
| High | Shakedowns and lockdown risk | Lay low, work shifts, attend everything |
| Critical | Guards actively target you | Abandon the tunnel for a day or two |

A few habits keep heat manageable:

- Never carry contraband through a checkpoint when you can stash it instead.
- Skip fights entirely in your first week — the attention isn't worth the loot.
- Attend every roll call and job shift, even when you'd rather be digging.
- Spread your digging across multiple nights so the noise and timing stay predictable.

**Community reports** from veteran players of the earlier games consistently point to the same failure pattern: new inmates dig too fast, too early, and get caught by a search they never saw coming. Slow and boring beats fast and caught.

## Your First Escape: A Step-by-Step Beginner Plan

The Escapists 3 beginner guide plan below works for almost any facility layout, because it's built around preparation rather than a specific route.

| Step | Action | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Learn the schedule and guard rotations | Never miss roll call |
| 2 | Take a job | Steady income for trades |
| 3 | Push one stat to a useful level | Unlock better crafting or combat |
| 4 | Scout the perimeter | Identify the least-guarded exit |
| 5 | Build a hidden stash | Keep tools safe from searches |
| 6 | Craft a decoy | Buy yourself extra hours |
| 7 | Execute during a quiet window | Escape without a chase |

Expanding on that sequence:

1. **Days one and two are pure observation.** Follow the routine perfectly and note when guard patrols thin out.
2. **Days three through five are investment.** Work shifts, train your chosen stat, and start collecting crafting materials.
3. **Days six and onward are execution.** Build the tunnel or disguise, assemble your kit, and keep heat low.
4. **Escape during free time or after lights out.** These windows give you the most minutes before anyone notices you're gone.

Finally, don't announce your plan to every inmate you meet. In a prison sandbox, information travels, and a loose-lipped neighbor can turn a clean escape into a lockdown before you've packed your stash.
