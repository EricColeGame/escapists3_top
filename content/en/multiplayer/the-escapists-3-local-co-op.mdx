## Split-Screen and Online: How The Escapists 3 Local Co Op Works

The two modes solve different problems. Split-screen is the pick for a living room full of people who want to shout over a shared plan. Online is the pick when your crew is spread across platforms and time zones, or when you want a full four-player team without crowding one screen.

One honest caveat: Team17 has confirmed split-screen but has not published an exact local player count. Community reports and forum chatter often assume four-player couch sessions, but treat that as unconfirmed until the studio publishes specifics. Online co-op for up to four players is the number that has been stated outright.

| Consideration | Local split-screen | Online co-op |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed player count | Not detailed publicly | Up to four players |
| Best for | Same-room sessions, fast reactions | Long-distance crews, flexible schedules |
| Communication | Face-to-face, no headset required | Voice chat strongly recommended |
| Screen space | Shared, so awareness drops | Full screen per player |
| Coordination speed | Fast and instinctive | Needs clear callouts and role discipline |
| Main drawback | Screen watching is harder | Plans drift if nobody speaks up |

Practical takeaway: pick the mode based on how much planning your group tolerates. A four-player online team can run separate tasks in parallel, while a split-screen group tends to work better when everyone stays in roughly the same area of the prison. That difference alone changes which escape routes are realistic.

## Four Inmates, One Plan: Roles That Keep Sessions Moving

The most common failure in a cooperative prison sandbox is not getting caught — it is four people doing the same job. Because The Escapists 3 local co op lets players split tasks, dividing responsibilities early prevents the classic deadlock where nobody has the item everyone needs.

| Role | Primary job | Key risk |
|---|---|---|
| Lookout | Track guard rotations and camera dead zones | Boredom leads to reckless wandering |
| Quartermaster | Trade with inmates and stockpile materials | Hoarding instead of sharing |
| Engineer | Craft tools and improvised equipment | Burning rare materials on failed attempts |
| Distraction | Draw guards away at the critical moment | Timing the diversion too early |
| Runner | Move contraband between cells and job sites | Getting searched mid-transit |

### A Simple Framework for a First Escape

You do not need a spreadsheet, but a loose sequence keeps a group aligned. This is a general framework, not a scripted route, since each prison has its own layout and rules.

| Phase | Focus | Team goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Recon | Watch routines, map patrol gaps | Everyone learns one route each |
| 2. Supply | Jobs, trades, and cell searches | Fill a shared stash of materials |
| 3. Craft | Build the tools your plan needs | Assign one crafter, not four |
| 4. Test | Poke at systems you plan to exploit | Confirm the timing works |
| 5. Execute | Run the escape together | One leader calls the go |

Player experience from earlier games in the series consistently points to the same lesson: the group that spends a full in-game day on recon escapes far more often than the group that starts digging on day one.

## Skills and Progression: What Carries Between Prisons

The Escapists 3 adds an XP system that unlocks additional skills and gameplay options. More importantly, progression carries over between prisons, letting you build an inmate around the escape methods you actually enjoy — brute force, stealth, crafting, or social manipulation. For a co-op group, that creates natural specialization.

| Player focus | Likely investment | Team benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Stealth | Quiet movement and timing tools | Fewer guard alerts during infiltration |
| Combat | Improvised weapons and toughness | A reliable fallback when plans collapse |
| Crafting | Better tools and resource efficiency | Fewer wasted materials per attempt |
| Social | Trading and inmate relationships | Faster access to rare items |

The practical consequence is that a four-player crew can cover all four lanes. Instead of everyone grinding the same tasks, each player invests in a lane the others are ignoring. When you move to a new prison with tougher restrictions, your existing skills soften the difficulty curve and let you experiment with approaches that would be too risky on a fresh save.

## Common Mistakes in The Escapists 3 Local Co Op Sessions

Even a well-organized crew can derail itself. These are the patterns worth watching for, along with fixes that cost nothing but a little discipline.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Everyone gathers the same materials | Duplicated effort, wasted in-game hours | Assign one material type per player |
| Ignoring routines | Guards rotate, and your route dies | Spend one day purely observing |
| No fallback plan | One alert ends the whole run | Agree on a hiding plan before you start |
| Hoarding tools | The team stalls while one player sits on items | Keep a shared stash near a cell block |
| Silent coordination | Plans drift within minutes | Talk constantly, even in split-screen |
| Chasing the perfect escape | Nobody ever pulls the trigger | Set a deadline and attempt it |

A useful rule of thumb: if your group has spent more time planning than playing, the plan is too complicated. The sandbox is designed so that improvisation is a legitimate strategy, not a failure state. Tools like pogo sticks and molten chocolate exist precisely because ridiculous solutions are supposed to work sometimes.

### Pre-Session Checklist

- Confirm which mode you are playing and how many players are joining.
- Test controllers and headsets before the session starts, not after.
- Assign the five roles above, even if you only have three players.
- Pick one prison and commit to it for the whole session.
- Agree on a rendezvous point for when things go wrong.
- Set a time limit so the group actually attempts the escape.
