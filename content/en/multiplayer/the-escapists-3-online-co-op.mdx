## Co-Op Modes Compared: Online, Split-Screen, and Solo

Choosing a mode changes how you play, not just who you play with. Solo runs reward patience and clean stealth routes. Split-screen co-op is built for two people sharing a couch and a single screen. Online co-op scales the chaos to four, which is where divided roles and real coordination start to pay off.

| Mode | Players | Best for | The trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | 1 | Learning routines and testing routes | Nobody covers your shift while you dig |
| Local split-screen | 2 | Couch sessions, quick escapes | Less screen space per player |
| Online co-op | Up to 4 | Full crews with divided jobs | Requires voice chat and timing discipline |
| Online PvP | Not detailed | Competitive play | Different objective entirely |
| Split-screen PvP | Not detailed | Local rivalry | Different objective entirely |

For a first escape attempt, two players is arguably the sweet spot: enough help to split chores, few enough people that nobody gets lost. Once your group knows a prison's schedule, four players becomes a genuine force multiplier — one person can run jobs for cash while another digs and a third keeps watch.

## Team Roles That Actually Work in a 4-Player Break

Four people improvising separately is just four separate failures. Four people with assigned jobs is a plan. In the previous games, the crews that escaped fastest were the ones who treated the prison like a project with workstreams. That logic carries straight into The Escapists 3.

| Role | Primary job | Useful kit | Why the crew needs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Digger | Opens the actual tunnel or breach | Shovel, pickaxe, light source | Progress is invisible until someone commits to it |
| The Quartermaster | Runs jobs, trades with inmates, stockpiles parts | Duct tape, files, scrap | Everything craftable starts as loot |
| The Lookout | Tracks guard patrols and roll call timing | Disguises, distraction items | Bad timing ruins good tunnels |
| The Face | Handles favors, bribes, and NPC relationships | Cash, trade goods | Reputation opens doors that crowbars can't |

Rotate roles between prisons. A player who spends one map digging should spend the next one running jobs, because the game's XP and skill system rewards broadening your inmate rather than locking into one trick.
