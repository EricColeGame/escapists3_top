Prison has rules. You have other plans — and that clash is the entire premise of **The Escapists 3 Team17**. The series that taught a generation to hide tunnels behind wall posters has gone fully 3D, and the sandbox is deeper than ever: five distinct prisons, 120+ craftable items and weapons, and multiplayer escapes for up to four inmates.

If you ever wondered whether a prison-break game could still surprise you after two installments, **The Escapists 3 Team17** answers with expanded movement, real vertical traversal, and a design philosophy that says there is no single correct solution — only good plans, bad plans, and the occasional plan involving molten chocolate.

## What The Escapists 3 Team17 Changes for the Series

Team17 Digital Ltd wears two hats on this project: it both publishes and develops the game. That matters, because the studio behind the earlier entries is steering the jump to 3D rather than handing the keys to someone else. And the shift is not cosmetic.

In a 3D prison sandbox, guards have sightlines instead of tiles. Walls have height. A route that looked safe in a top-down view can be completely exposed from a watchtower. Players coming from the older games will feel the difference within their first in-game day.

Here is the feature set at a glance, based on the [official Xbox Store listing for The Escapists 3](https://www.xbox.com/en-US/games/store/the-escapists-3/9nkkw5b5pkv7):

| Platforms | Xbox Series X and Series S, PC |
| Prisons | Five distinct facilities |
| Craftables | 120+ items and weapons |

The practical takeaway: the 3D format gives you more ways to move, more ways to be seen, and more ways to cause trouble. Both of those things are true at once, and learning to balance them is the core skill of the game.

## Five Prisons, Five Systems to Outsmart

Every facility in **The Escapists 3 Team17** runs on a routine. Guards rotate posts, work shifts open and close, and meal times create windows of predictable chaos. That schedule is the real lock you are picking — learn it once and the entire prison becomes a machine you can exploit.

Veterans of the series tend to follow the same opening sequence. Community reports consistently recommend treating the first two in-game days as pure reconnaissance rather than attempting anything clever.

| What to Observe | Why It Matters | Typical Timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Guard patrol routes | Reveals blind spots and shift changes | Days 1–2 |
| Job openings | Income plus access to restricted areas | Day 1 |
| Inmate traders | Sources for items you cannot craft yet | Days 2–3 |
| Cell search chances | Free materials and contraband | Ongoing, low risk |
| Weak walls and floors | The start of any digging or climbing route | Days 3+ |

The five prisons each carry their own characters, secrets, and opportunities to slip past guards, so a plan that works in one facility will rarely transfer cleanly to the next. That is intentional: the game wants you to re-learn the system every time.

### Beyond the Walls

Escaping the cell block is not always the finish line. According to the official description, the world outside holds its own obstacles — and this is where the 3D sandbox earns its keep.

| Environment | Obstacle Type | What Players Suggest Bringing |

Player experience suggests that many failed escapes end not at the wall but *past* it, when an inmate runs out of supplies halfway through the wilderness. Pack for the second leg of the journey, not just the first.

## Escape Routes and Crafting: Turning Junk Into Freedom

The crafting system is where **The Escapists 3 Team17** gets genuinely creative. With more than 120 items and weapons to loot, trade, and craft, almost anything can become part of an escape plan — whether it should is a separate question entirely.

There are several broad route archetypes, and most successful escapes combine at least two of them.

| Approach | What You Need | Best For | Main Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Digging | Digging tools and tunnel cover | Long-term, planned escapes | Noise and tunnel discovery |
| Sneaking | Schedule knowledge, quiet movement | Reaching walls undetected | Patrol timing |
| Climbing | Climbing gear, stamina | Walls and vertical gaps | Falls and exposure |
| Fighting | Weapons, physical stats | Last-resort breaks | Injuries, lockdown |
| Disguise | Uniforms and forged credentials | Walking out the front | Suspicion from guards |
| Improvising | Everyday junk plus crafting | One-of-a-kind routes | Unpredictable outcomes |

Supplies come from three main channels: searching cells, trading with other inmates, and completing jobs. Each has a trade-off. Searching is free but slow. Trading is fast but costs cash. Jobs pay well and open doors, but they also put you on the schedule where guards can watch you.

| Item Role | Typical Source | Example Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Digging tools | Crafted from scavenged parts | Tunnels beneath cell walls |
| Climbing gear | Trading or job rewards | Scaling walls and canyon faces |
| Disguises | Stolen uniforms, crafted credentials | Passing guards at close range |
| Weapons | Crafted or traded | Surviving a fight you did not want |
| Distractions | Everyday junk | Pulling a guard off a post |

Community players often note that the best item in the game is usually the boring one — the tool that lets you stay hidden for one more day rather than the weapon that ends a fight you should have avoided.

## Co-Op, Crossplay, and Playing With Accomplices

Solo escapes are satisfying, but **The Escapists 3 Team17** was clearly built with accomplices in mind. You can play alone, team up with a friend on the couch via split-screen, or take the escape online with up to four players using crossplay.

| Mode | Player Count | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo | 1 | Learning routines quietly and testing routes |
| Local split-screen | 2 | Couch co-op planning and shared screens |
| Online multiplayer | 2–4 | Role-splitting, crossplay, coordinated breaks |

In a coordinated group, the smart play is to divide labor. One inmate farms cash through jobs, another handles crafting, a third maps guard rotations, and the fourth keeps the tunnel covered. Coordinating timings is the hard part — a dig that finishes two minutes early is a dig that gets found.

The game's own marketing sums it up nicely: escaping together is the fastest way to discover which of your friends is useless with a shovel.

## A Practical Escape Checklist for New Inmates

If you are starting fresh in **The Escapists 3 Team17**, work through this sequence rather than improvising from day one.

| Phase | Action | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recon | Watch guard rotations for two full in-game days | High |
| Income | Take a job and bank your first cash | High |
| Supply | Search your cell and open trade with inmates | High |
| Craft | Build the first tool for your chosen route | High |
| Cover | Hide your tunnel, stash, or contraband | Medium |
| Execute | Move during a predictable window such as roll call | Critical |

A few habits separate smooth escapes from short ones:

- **Never dig without cover.** A tunnel is only useful if nobody finds it.
- **Bank cash early.** Traders solve problems that scavenging cannot.
- **Practice in one prison first.** Skills and XP carry forward, so early investment pays off later.
- **Customize your inmate around your route.** If you plan to climb, build for climbing.
- **Treat the wilderness as a second level.** Supplies for the outside matter as much as tools for the inside.

XP unlocks new skills and gameplay options, and what you learn in one facility carries into the next — so even a failed escape is progress.

## FAQ: The Escapists 3 Team17 Questions Answered

**Who develops and publishes The Escapists 3 Team17?**

Team17 Digital Ltd handles both roles, serving as developer and publisher of the game.

**How many players can escape together?**

Two players can team up in local split-screen co-op, while online multiplayer supports up to four players with crossplay enabled.

**How many prisons are included?**

There are five distinct prisons, each with its own routines, characters, secrets, and opportunities to sneak past the guards.

**Is there one correct escape route?**

No. The game is built around player choice — digging, sneaking, climbing, fighting, disguising, and improvising are all valid, and most escapes combine several approaches. That flexibility is exactly what makes **The Escapists 3 Team17** worth replaying.
