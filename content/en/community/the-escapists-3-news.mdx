## Why Going 3D Is a Bigger Deal Than It Sounds

The Escapists has always been a game about information. You learn where the guards walk, when the roll call happens, which inmate will trade you duct tape, and how long a shovel takes to break. All of that knowledge is built on a fixed top-down camera that lets you see the whole room at once.

Switch to a 3D perspective and that knowledge economy changes. Sightlines become a resource. Corners hide things. A camera that swings behind your character means you can no longer read the entire yard in a single glance, which is either the best thing to happen to the series or the fastest way to break it.

Here is how the shift could reshape the core loop, based on how the previous games worked:

| Design element | In the 2D games | If The Escapists 3 goes 3D |
|---|---|---|
| Guard patrols | Read at a glance from above | Must be learned through observation and memory |
| Hiding and cover | Limited by tile placement | Real verticality, corners, and sightlines |
| Crafting and desks | Menu-driven, always visible | Risk of hiding systems behind camera angles |
| Heists and escapes | Puzzle-like, top-down routing | More physical, more prone to jank |
| Multiplayer chaos | Easy to read, easy to laugh at | Harder to coordinate, potentially funnier |

That last row matters more than it looks. The Escapists 2 turned the formula into a genuinely great co-op game, and any change in perspective has to preserve the ability to shout "meet me at the vent in ten seconds" and have it actually work.
