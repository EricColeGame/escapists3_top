## The Prep Loop: Planning an Escape That Survives Roll Call

No escape method works without preparation, and the daily routine is where most breakouts die. Expect the same rhythm as before: attend everything, stay on the right side of the guards, and spend every free minute on your plan.

| Time block | Priority | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Morning roll call | Be present, be on time | Absences raise suspicion and tighten security |
| Work shift | Farm job-specific materials | Jobs are the cleanest source of tools |
| Free period | Trade, craft, and scout | The only window for social and recon work |
| Exercise yard | Build strength and contacts | Unlocks favors and physical routes |
| Evening lockdown | Dig, cut, or forge | Cover of darkness, but guards sweep more often |
| Lights out | Sleep or push your luck | Exhaustion causes mistakes during the escape |

A practical rule that carried players through the earlier games: never start an escape on the same day you finish preparing it. Bank one full day of normal behavior first. If a guard noticed a missing spoon or a strange smell, that buffer gives the heat time to cool.

## Common Mistakes That Sink Otherwise Good Escapes

Most failed breakouts aren't caused by bad plans — they're caused by impatience. Here's the short list of habits worth breaking before 2027.

| Mistake | What happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Digging every night | Fatigue and missed roll calls | Alternate digging with rest days |
| Ignoring guard suspicion | Cell shakedowns and confiscations | Keep contraband split across hiding spots |
| Hoarding one tool type | A single search wipes your progress | Diversify across two escape methods |
| Escaping without a fallback | One blocked route ends the run | Always have a secondary plan half-built |
| Skipping favors | No intel, no distractions | Trade early and often |
| Testing the perimeter too soon | Security posture hardens | Scout from a distance first |

The Escapists 3 escape methods will almost certainly punish tunnel vision — literally and figuratively. Building toward two routes at once costs more materials but keeps you alive when the first one collapses.
